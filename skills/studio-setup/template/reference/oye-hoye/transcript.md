## Full script (English)

In 2016, this brand was on its way to becoming Pakistan's number one snack brand. But then it vanished, as if no one had ever known it. This is the story of Oye Hoye chips. In 2015, United Snacks set up a factory near Lahore, and imported modern machines from Europe and Turkey, for quality on par with Lay's. They named it: Oye Hoye! Simple, desi, and memorable. Fawad Khan became the brand ambassador. From TV to billboards, Oye Hoye was everywhere. Sales boomed so much that even Lay's sales started to fall. Then the war reached the shop counter. According to industry estimates, Lay's started giving shopkeepers one free carton for every ten, and for keeping it on the top shelf, a monthly reward as well. At the same time, rumours began to spread in the market: "It's not halal." "The flavour smells." But putting all the blame on Lay's would be wrong. Oye Hoye's distribution was weak. People saw the ads, but couldn't find the chips in shops. And when sales didn't grow as expected, the company shut down marketing altogether. The ads stopped, and people forgot the brand. Creating hype is easy. Keeping it alive is hard. Oye Hoye is still found in a few shops today, but its magic is now just a memory.
